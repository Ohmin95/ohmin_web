I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Edward S. Rogers Sr. Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering at the University of Toronto, working with Prof. Wei Yu. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from KAIST in 2026 and my M.S. from UNIST in 2023, both under the supervision of Prof. Jinseok Choi, and my B.S. in Electronics Engineering from Jeonbuk National University in 2021.

My current research interests include beamforming algorithm design for multi-user MIMO systems—in particular, scalable and low-complexity precoding for hybrid, RIS-aided, and coarsely quantized massive MIMO architectures under imperfect channel state information—as well as an analysis of cell-free MIMO under limited fronthaul capacity and the design of AI-aided Kalman filtering algorithms for unknown dynamics.

Research

  • MU-MIMO beamforming — low-complexity and scalable beamforming design for massive MIMO with hybrid architectures and low-resolution ADCs/DACs; secure precoding and beamforming design for finite blocklength communications.
  • RIS-aided networks — phase-shift optimization design for multi-RIS systems under imperfect channel state information, and channel estimation and for RIS with low-resolution phase shifters, where discrete phase-shift optimization is recast as a continuous problem via Bussgang-based latent lifting.
  • Cell-free MIMO — channel estimation under limited fronthaul capacity, where pilot contamination is incurred deliberately as a form of CSI compression: reusing pilots reduces the CSI dimension each access point must forward, and the released fronthaul budget pays for finer quantization.
  • AI-aided Kalman filtering — task-aware neural Kalman filtering for unknown dynamics, where both the state predictor and the Kalman gain are learned jointly from data without knowledge of the state-evolution function or noise statistics.
